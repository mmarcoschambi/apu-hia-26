Only buy stocks in confirmed Stage 2 uptrends. The ideal setup is a stock emerging from a proper base after a period of consolidation, showing a constructive price structure with contracting volatility.2. The best stocks to buy are those making new 52-week highs with strong fundamentals backing the move. Minervini looks for stocks breaking out from sound bases, not those rallying from oversold conditions.3. Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP) is the cornerstone setup. Look for a series of contracting price oscillations—each pullback should be shallower than the last, creating a tightening pattern that precedes explosive moves.4. Volume should dry up during the contraction phase and expand on the breakout. The ideal pattern shows declining volume as the stock tightens, then surges of volume 40-50% above average on the breakout day.5. Buy as close to the pivot point as possible, ideally within 1-5% of the proper buy point. The further you chase above the pivot, the worse your risk/reward becomes. Wait for the next setup if you miss the entry.6. Risk management is non-negotiable: Never risk more than 1-1.5% of total equity on any single trade. Cut losses quickly at 7-8% maximum from your entry point. Most of Minervini's losses are held to 3-5%.7. The leading stocks emerge from the best bases after healthy pullbacks of 15-35%. Shallow pullbacks of 10-12% often produce the strongest moves, but the key is the quality of the consolidation structure, not just the depth.8. Relative strength is critical—focus on stocks outperforming the market. Minervini's screening criteria includes stocks above their 200-day and 50-day moving averages, with the 50-day above the 200-day, and the 200-day trending up for at least one month.9. The general market direction determines your success rate. Don't fight the overall market trend. When the major indexes are in confirmed downtrends, move to cash. During market corrections, preservation of capital is the priority.10. Fundamental earnings growth must support the technical setup. Look for accelerating earnings growth, strong ROE, increasing institutional sponsorship, and a compelling industry story. The ideal stock shows both earnings and sales accelerating quarter-over-quarter.11. Position sizing should reflect conviction and market conditions. In strong bull markets with multiple setups working, you can be more aggressive. In choppy markets, reduce size and selectivity becomes paramount. Pyramid into winners, never losers.12. The best performers often show similar characteristics: They're part of a leading industry group, have a stock price of at least $15-20, show tight weekly closes, exhibit constructive action on down days in the market, and demonstrate sponsorship by top-performing mutual funds.The Four-Stage Market CycleMinervini's framework is built around understanding where a stock sits in its lifecycle:Stage 1 (Neglect/Basing): Sideways consolidation after a decline. Not tradeable yet.Stage 2 (Advance): The profitable phase. Stock breaks out and trends higher with expanding volume.Stage 3 (Top/Distribution): Sideways movement after an advance. Time to take profits.Stage 4 (Decline): Downtrend. Avoid entirely or short only if that's your methodology.Core Philosophy"The whole secret to winning in the stock market is to lose the least amount possible when you're not right." Minervini's exceptional track record (an average annual return of 33.6% over five years in the U.S. Investing Championship, with a peak year of 155% in 1997) stems from excellent defense combined with letting winners run.Price and volume tell you everything you need to know. While fundamentals identify which stocks to watch, the tape tells you when to buy and sell. A stock's price action reflects all known and unknown information.Patience is the key to getting proper entries. Wait for the setup to fully develop. Most traders fail because they buy too early in the base or chase stocks too far extended from proper buy points.The stock doesn't know you own it. Don't get emotionally attached. If the setup breaks, exit immediately. The market doesn't care about your entry price or your opinion.Successful trading is about process, not outcomes. You can execute perfectly and still lose on individual trades.
